Everything About Laser Resurfacing

What is Laser Resurfacing?

Laser skin resurfacing is a medical aesthetic procedure that uses concentrated beams of light energy to remove damaged skin layers precisely, cell by cell. It simultaneously triggers the skin's natural wound-healing response, stimulating the production of new collagen and elastin fibres.

At DermaCare, we use a combination of two gold-standard technologies to customise your treatment based on your unique skin profile and aesthetic goals.

Suitable Skin Types

This treatment is suitable for Fitzpatrick skin types I–IV. Patients with darker skin tones may be candidates for modified protocols. A thorough assessment by our dermatologist will determine the most appropriate laser type, wavelength, and intensity for your skin.

Ideal candidates include individuals concerned about fine lines, wrinkles, sun damage, acne scarring, uneven pigmentation, enlarged pores, or general skin laxity.

What Happens During Your Session?

1
Consultation & Skin Assessment

Your dermatologist reviews your medical history, assesses your skin type using the Fitzpatrick scale, and photographs your treatment area for before/after comparison. Treatment parameters are discussed and confirmed.

2
Cleansing & Preparation

Your skin is thoroughly cleansed to remove all makeup, oils, and impurities. A sterile draping is applied around the treatment zone to maintain a clean field.

3
Topical Numbing Cream Application

Medical-grade topical anaesthetic (lidocaine 4%) is applied to the treatment area and left for 30–45 minutes to ensure your comfort throughout the procedure.

4
Protective Eye Shields Applied

Protective eye shields are carefully placed to protect your eyes from the laser energy. You may feel slight pressure but no discomfort.

5
Fractional Laser Passes

Your dermatologist systematically passes the laser handpiece over the treatment area in overlapping rows. You may feel a warm, tingling sensation. The laser creates thousands of microscopic columns in the skin, stimulating intense healing activity.

6
Cooling & Calming Treatment

A cooling device or cold compress is applied immediately after the laser to calm the skin, reduce redness, and minimise discomfort. A soothing serum is then gently applied.

7
Aftercare Application & Instructions

A barrier-protecting post-laser moisturiser and broad-spectrum SPF 50+ are applied. Your nurse provides detailed written aftercare instructions and your recovery kit to take home.

Recovery Timeline

Day 1–3: Redness & Warmth

Expect significant redness (similar to a sunburn), warmth, and mild swelling — especially around the eyes. Cold compresses and prescribed barrier creams provide comfort. Social downtime recommended.

Day 4–7: Peeling & Bronzing

Skin begins to bronze and peel as treated columns shed. Do not pick or rub — allow the skin to shed naturally. Keep skin hydrated with prescribed emollients and avoid makeup.

Week 2: Visible Improvement

Redness fades to a soft pink. New skin feels smoother and looks noticeably brighter. Most patients feel comfortable returning to work with mineral makeup. Continued SPF 50+ use is essential.

Month 1: Progressive Collagen Building

Collagen remodelling continues over 4–6 weeks. Texture and tone improvements become increasingly apparent. The full effect of each session reveals itself progressively.

Month 2–6: Full Results

After completing all recommended sessions, you will enjoy the full cumulative benefit: significantly smoother, tighter, more radiant skin with diminished scarring, pigmentation, and wrinkles.

Recovery Tips
  • Apply SPF 50+ sunscreen every 2 hours outdoors
  • Keep skin continuously moisturised with prescribed emollient
  • Avoid direct sun exposure for 4 weeks post-treatment
  • Do not pick, rub, or exfoliate during the peeling phase
  • Avoid heat — saunas, hot yoga — for 7–10 days
  • Sleep elevated (extra pillow) for the first 3 nights
  • Stay well-hydrated and eat an antioxidant-rich diet
